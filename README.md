# EPISTASSI — THE LISTENING / THE THIRTEEN CUT

A first-person psychological horror investigation set in a fictional stretch of the Po Valley. Thirteen people have gathered around an unheard record called **The White**. They want to empty themselves so that something can occupy the space they leave behind. The record is never played. The game asks whether there was ever an album to hear, or whether the room, its machinery and the people waiting inside it became the recording.

The atmosphere combines industrial decay, domestic ritual, damaged Catholic forms, winter fields, damp interiors and brief images that surface like involuntary memories. The horror is built around attention: what the player watches, what they choose not to interrupt, and the moment an image becomes evidence.

## The central ambiguity

The winter 1977 report lists thirteen deaths in a country church at 03:17. A portable gas heater, a sealed room and a blocked air intake suggest a possible ventilation failure. The coroner's copy still says **cause undetermined**. Carbon monoxide is a theory within the story, not a confirmed solution.

The empty reel transport turns without tape. Its VU meters do not rise. Ottavio, who is deaf, claims to have kept the only copy of the record. The room may have been recorded as sound; it may be what remains when sound is removed. The dead cannot verify either account.

The cult has made a theology out of an absence. Nietzsche's sentence about willing nothingness appears in a battered paperback, but the timings in its margins belong to The First. A second line has been added by hand: *What is falling must also be pushed.* The book does not identify who pushed anyone. The margin lets the reader see how responsibility can be disguised as interpretation.

## Story route

1. **The farmhouse.** Meet Ines and Ottavio before examining the evidence they keep. Ines's warmth is not fanaticism: her thermos connects the house to Gabri's death, and she remains the person who can still care for another body. Ottavio is deaf, yet insists he listened by placing his jaw against the table and feeling the motor stop.
2. **The wall.** After speaking with both of them, listen through the thin partition. A voice gives Dario a task. Dario asks where to stand. Tobia hears the instruction and does not open the door. That silence is recorded as an action in the journal.
3. **The grave and the church.** Recover the name beneath the willow, question the Widow and speak with Don Remo. He continues to wear the collar and perform the gestures of a vocation whose addressee no longer answers. The altar's instructions are deliberately deceptive. The bell sequence makes the player's hand part of Dario's martyrdom: Walter carries out the order while Tobia keeps the rope moving.
4. **The dark archive.** Three altar candles open the cellar's side door. The dark room is a maze of chairs, tables, partitions and papers. The flashlight reveals six records: a radiant gas heater, wax earplugs, The First's paperback, an empty reel transport, the coroner's copy and fourteen invented signs scratched into the plaster. The heater report remains inconclusive.
5. **The slaughterhouse.** A bedsheet, a 16 mm projector and thirteen seats turn the room into a private cinema. The player must take a seat and watch a silent collapse. A child claps before the body reaches the floor. The image arrives first; the sound comes later. The screening is required before the final platter can be heard.
6. **The tea at 03:17.** Return to The First after searching the archive. The First explains why he wore the earplugs: he wanted to keep the image without hearing the sound. Ines brings the tea. The First watches the dust settle. His fall appears before its sound; Ines remains, and the house still has someone to open the door for the next arrival.
7. **The platter.** With the eight main clues, the altar rejected, the dark archive searched, The First's tea scene witnessed and the slaughterhouse screening completed, the player can approach The White. The ending does not resolve whether the room was the recording or merely the place where people learned to hear one.

After the forest chase, an optional **Pastorale** trail can be recovered at the paddy, grain field and canal reeds. The Mass has contracted to a few gestures; mourning moves out of the church and into water, crops and trees. No funeral is held. The landscape inherits the interval. The fourth, testimony ending is available only after the grave name and all three pastoral intervals have been recorded.

## The people

- **Ines** serves warmth without demanding belief. She carried the thermos to Gabri, notices Tobia's nosebleed and wipes it without asking what caused it. After The First's death she remains, making care the institution's last surviving function.
- **Ottavio** is deaf, yet the only person entrusted with the record. His evidence cannot be checked by anyone else.
- **Il Professore** (Corrado Vesci, called *The First* in older builds) is a changing presence: welcoming at the porch, evasive in the slaughterhouse, and associated with the dark archive. His language borrows philosophy and turns it into a way to watch without hearing.
- **Dario** believes in The White more sincerely than the others. His faith makes him useful to the people who need a martyr.
- **Walter** turns obedience into an identity. He does not need to decide what the task means.
- **Pino** doubts the rite and runs toward the wheat field. The combine follows him; the sound of the collapse arrives after the image.
- **Anna** keeps serving an empty place. Her soup is set over a trowel, placing nourishment and the disturbance of a grave beneath the same table.
- **Marta** writes memoirs in a sunshine that was never there. Her false golden memory is punctured by the body's involuntary signs.
- **Gabri** wanted to be seen. In the car, he mistook the thermos for a gift.
- **Sergio** stayed for company after he stopped believing. He heard the plan for Dario and did not warn him.
- **Livia** lost her speech but kept the melody. **Franco** turns butchery into liturgy. **Don Remo** keeps the gestures of a vocation after faith has emptied out.

## Props and spaces

The world uses recognisable objects rather than generic display blocks wherever the object carries story weight: a 1970s saloon car, an enamel thermos, a radiant LPG heater with ceramic panels and hose, wax earplugs in a brass case, a reel-to-reel transport, a 16 mm projector, a cast-metal church bell with clapper and rope, a turntable cabinet, a trowel, tableware, wooden chairs and a rusted combine harvester. The dark archive and the slaughterhouse use damp plaster, brick, timber, metal and paper to keep the spaces materially grounded.

The four slaughterhouse forms are sculptural and stylised rather than anatomically graphic. The game's visual language is intentionally degraded: film grain, brief flash images, worn materials, fog, unstable light, and a camera that sometimes behaves like an observer rather than a neutral first-person view.

## Version 32 — sooner, one at a time, apart

- **The dresser has a light inside.** Opening the door brings up a small warm light in the cupboard (and dims with the door), so the jars, the tin and Walter's keys on their hook can be seen; the keys hang nearer the door and catch the light.
- **Pursuers no longer stick together.** When two or more of the envious follow Tobia outside, they come in at different points around the entrance, keep their distance from each other (closest measured: 2.9 m), and come at him from different sides until they are close.
- **They snap a little sooner, and one at a time.** The grace of real play after the introduction is shorter (2½ minutes at Ascolto, 1⅔ at Messa, a little over 1 at Il Bianco), and the readings need less (six things examined, two clues found, 55 seconds sitting still). But two never turn together: after one snaps, the house has at least 3⅓ minutes at Ascolto (less at higher difficulty) before the next, and the no-side/high pressures wait two minutes after any snap.
- **By the sixth clue, at the latest,** someone has turned, at every difficulty, wherever Tobia is (outside, it comes as a voice behind him: "That was mine to find").

## Version 31 — the cigarette, the table, relics, and reasons

**Il Primo's cigarette.** The cigarette is no longer fixed to the wrist (which held it across the mouth). It is placed from the body every frame, the way people hold one: between the index and middle fingers just below the knuckles; at the lips the filter rests in the mouth and the cigarette points straight out, perpendicular to the face and tilted about 12° down; between draws it rides between the fingers pointing forward and down, ember away from the body. The arm bends at the elbow first so the hand comes up close along the body, and the shoulder follows. Measured at the mouth: 12° from the face's forward axis, filter 0.7 cm from the lips.

**Standing up angry.** Someone who snaps at the table no longer walks across it: from a chair they push back and stand out on their own side; standing elsewhere, they come toward Tobia only as far as the floor is clear.

**Posters as relics.** Ten of the fifteen posters are now off the walls — on the floor by the door, slid under the harpsichord, folded under the bread on the kitchen table, on the table in the empty house, by drains, among chairs — and the five still on walls hang from a nail. All are old: foxed, creased, the ink sunk into sepia paper, and torn from a nibbled edge to most of the sheet gone (the torn paper is cut out, with a pale fibrous rim along each rip). Their descriptions are fragments, not explanations. AB DEDUS SPIEGL CABDAMION stays legible on its one poster.

**Why they snap.** Nobody turns on Tobia on a timer any more, and never right after the introduction: there is a grace of real play first (4 minutes at Ascolto, 2½ at Messa, about 1½ at Il Bianco). Then the house reads what he does, the way the faithful read everything — as Il Bianco choosing him — and the reading is shown before it turns into anything:
- **examining things** (walls, papers, posters, belongings): "Gabri has been watching you read the house. Every time you stop at something, he stops eating." → "You look at everything… like it was written for you. Like it is talking to you."
- **sitting still** for a minute: "Dario is watching you sit there doing nothing…" → "A whole minute… Only someone listening sits like that. Tell me what it sounds like."
- **going out alone**, again and again: they look at the mud on your shoes → "Where do you go in the dark? The church? On your own?"
- **finding things**: their face does something when they see what you carry → "That was mine to find."
- **whispering with someone they despise**: → "You keep going back to Sergio… Who it is you are going to tell?" (a traitor)
- **circling the house without speaking to anyone**: → "You walk round and round us… like you are working out which chair is yours."
First comes the noticing (a line, a cold stare); if Tobia keeps on, that same person snaps, says the reason, and then who they are ("Eleven years I have knelt at that turntable…"). The other pressures (no side, high) also wait until the grace is over.

## Version 30 — posters, papers, the dresser

**Posters.** Every poster in the game is now one of the real covers in `assets/inspiration`, printed as a gig poster for the house's own bands: the cover, the name, a small venue line, paper grain and folds, sun-faded or damp where they hang. AB DEDUS SPIEGL CABDAMION (the winged altarpiece creature) is on one poster only, on the east wall of the farmhouse, and the words are spoken aloud exactly once in a whole game, by one member, under their breath. THE ENDLESS EPHEMERAL (the worm in thorns), SANTGROUSE (the two surgical profiles), SATRAGNOSIUM (the opened egg creature), LITHOGON MOLENS INFANTIUM (the devouring giant), and an unnamed one with its own unreadable logo (the moss figure) are on the other walls and floors.

**Music.** After seven clues the outside world (road and forest) switches to `krautmeth.m4a`, a darker piece; it starts at once if the seventh clue is found outdoors.

**Skippable introduction.** The presentations at the farmhouse table can be skipped with Space: Tobia ends up seated and the journal entry about Il Bianco is kept, as if the scene had played.

**The dresser.** The wooden block by Gabri's corner is now a two-part Po-valley kitchen dresser (credenza con alzata, first half of the twentieth century): a stained-poplar base cabinet about 92 cm high and 55 cm deep with two drawers over two raised-panel doors on iron hinges, a worktop with a moulded edge; above it, set back, a 32 cm deep plate rack behind two glazed doors with plates and cups on rails, under a stepped cornice; white ceramic knobs. Walter's van keys are no longer in a saucer on top: they hang on a cup hook inside the cupboard, behind the right-hand lower door, and you have to open the door to find them.

**Clues you read.** The paper clues open full screen as the paper itself when you pick them up (and again from the journal): the provincial newspaper page of 19 January 1977, Ines's seating list on squared notebook paper with the scored fourteenth line legible in raking light, the burned photograph front and back, a page of the register of stations with red rubrics, and the slaughterhouse loading ledger. What is written on them is not said anywhere else in the house. E, Esc, Enter or a click puts the paper down.

## Version 29.2 — envy

**In every game, someone envies Tobia.** It no longer matters whether he steals, lies or takes a side. The house notices that he is acting strangely: he listens at doors, he finds things, the record seems to speak to him. To the faithful this is the worst thing a stranger can do: reach Il Bianco before them. One of them turns, at the table, in a short scene that does not hide the anger. Dario stands too fast and the chair goes over ("A week. You have been here a week, and it speaks to you"); Marta has the burin shaking in her fist; Walter speaks very quietly, which is worse. The zealots go first (Dario, Marta, Walter, Gabri, Anna, Franco, Sergio, Pino, Ines).

From then on they follow him **everywhere except the farmhouse**. In the farmhouse they sit at the table and stare, cold with anger, and do nothing. Outside — the road, the church, the cellar, the empty house, the slaughterhouse — they come after him and try to **stab him** (the knife reflex, from behind: A/D to get out of the way, E for the arm). They cannot be talked down: face to face they say it to you ("It was supposed to be me. Do you understand? Me."), and at the strike they scream it. Surviving does not end it: they withdraw, and come back.

**The closer Tobia gets to the truth, the worse it is.** Each clue, the altar, the dark room, the collapse, the forest: the envious get faster and bolder, a voice comes from the dark ("That one was mine to find."), and more of the house turns.

**Difficulty.** At 1 (Ascolto) the first turns within about two minutes and a second by the fifth clue; at 2 (Messa) sooner, faster, and they keep coming slowly even while looked at; at 3 (Il Bianco) from the start, faster still, up to four of them, recovering quickly after a failed attempt. The reflex window narrows with difficulty (0.42 s at 1, 0.34 s at 2, 0.28 s at 3). At 0 (Pastorale), as before, they cannot go through with it.

## Version 29.1 — the road runs smoother

Outdoors the road zone carried 29 lights (lamps along the lane, the industrial cemetery, the church front, the porch) and five shadow-casting lights. Most of the lamps are 30–45 m from the player and reach only 5–18 m, but every light was still evaluated for every pixel on screen. Now only the nearest few of each kind stay on (6 point lights, 3 spotlights, 2 shadow-casting spotlights); a lamp that is too far is swapped for one that is near, and because the number kept per kind never changes, the shaders are never recompiled while walking about (no stutter). Shadow maps are redrawn every other frame (every frame during cutscenes). Measured in the test renderer at the farmyard: 29 → 14 active lights, 3,747 → 2,591 draw calls, frame time down by about a third; the house improves similarly (6,177 → 3,441 draw calls).

## Version 29 — people who could kill you

Nobody in the house is an enemy. They are people who have decided something about Tobia, for a reason with a history, and who go on cutting bread and complaining about the coffee while they wait for the room to empty. When they act, they act with what is already in their hands. The new code is in `js/violence.js`, built on the relationships, motives and stalking already in `js/society.js`; the body-level effects (reflexes, the wound, poisoning, objects in hands, attack poses) are a small "peril" toolkit in `js/game.js`.

**Hostility persists.** Ignoring someone, or standing in front of them saying nothing, or walking away mid-encounter no longer resolves anything: it makes it worse (their grievance rises, the house records that you walked out on them), and they try again later. Leaving the room interrupts an encounter but not its cause: through the door you hear the knife go back to the board, faster. A person who is watched does not vanish any more; they walk away out of sight. Their absence shows: an empty chair with a full glass, someone asking where they went, the back door open, an empty slot in the knife block, the van parked somewhere nobody parks it.

**Character threats.**
- *Franco — the butcher's knife.* He goes to the board in the kitchen to cut the Sunday joint, and the knife is in his hand from then on (the big slot in the knife block is empty). When the kitchen empties — Gabri by the fridge does not count, nobody counts Gabri — he comes across the room talking about the marbling, stops too close, raises the knife slowly while he talks, cocks it back, and thrusts. He follows if you back off; he does not follow out of the room.
- *Pino — the pipe wrench.* He takes the toolbox out to the van. When Tobia is in the yard he puts himself between him and the farmhouse door, smashes the van's own glass first ("Look what you made me do"), then swings with all his weight: a long wind-up behind the shoulder, the body turning.
- *Ines — the tea.* She fills the kettle, warms the pot, does something with the cup with her back turned (you see her hand go into her apron pocket only if you are watching), brings it, holds it out, and waits. Drink it, sip it, smell it, ask her to have one with you, swap the cups while she looks at the door, or put it down. Nothing on screen says it is poisoned: the cues are hers (she has not poured one for herself; it is the chipped cup from the dead man's place; she watches your mouth). If you drink it, your lips go numb, the room goes far away and soft, your hands are slow, your nose bleeds; you have about a minute to get it out of you over the kitchen basin, and if she is near she holds your hair and tells you that you ate too fast. A fictional, unnamed poison.
- *Walter — the cesspit.* He takes the concrete lid off the cesspit in the yard, on the way from the door to the van, props it against the van wheel and lays black silage plastic and straw over the hole. The yard smells of drains; in the torchlight the plastic sags in the middle; you can lift the sheet. If you fall, your ankle goes, the sky is a small circle, you climb rung by rung — and if Walter is near he comes to the edge, says he'll get a rope, and starts sliding the lid back over.
- *The van.* Pino's (or Walter's) van is moved to where nobody parks it, nose toward the yard. When Tobia is in front of it the starter turns over, the headlights come on, the engine revs, and it comes in a straight line. Step out of its line early, or throw yourself aside at the last moment.
- The others keep their own ways: Gabri's bread knife from behind his back in the fridge light; Dario's rosary at the turntable; Walter's detonation lead at the church; Marta, Anna and the stalkers from behind. Not everyone attacks: Livia warns on napkins, Ottavio feels it through the floor, Sergio defends his friend, Anna pretends nothing happened.

**Reflexes.** Every attack is staged so it can be read — the knife going up and back, the wrench behind the shoulder, a breath and a rustle behind you, the starter motor — and then there is one short window in which the body can do something appropriate: A/D to step out of a thrust or E to catch the wrist; C to get under a swing or S to step back (not if there is a wall behind you); S to drive back into someone behind you or E to get your fingers under the cord; A/D to throw yourself out of a van's path; E to let go of the lead. Too early is a flinch that costs a moment; too late hurts (a cut forearm, a shoulder that puts you on your knees, a hip clipped by a wing mirror); nothing kills. The window is about 0.4 s at the default difficulty (Ascolto), wider at Pastorale (where, as before, the attacker cannot go through with it), narrower at Messa and Il Bianco. A faint hint appears only the first time each kind of danger happens.

**The wound.** Afterwards the body keeps it: a tremor in the view, a heartbeat you can hear, breath that catches, sound going far away when the person who did it is near; the first time you see them again, a fragment of the moment comes back. A leg that will not take weight after a fall or a blow. It fades, but not while they are alive and in the room. The one who did it has a state of their own — resentful, humiliated, ashamed, afraid, satisfied — and goes back to something ordinary too quickly: pulling the knife out of the door frame and complaining about the door.

**The table.** Everyone heard something; nobody heard the same thing. Each person takes a position from who they are and who they protect: the attacker's friends defend them ("You don't go near a man when he's working"), the ones who distort accuse you, the careful ones move their plates away from both of you, the ones who protect you warn you later, privately, and the violent and loyal quietly encourage it, in plain sight, at the sink. Then, at once, the ordinary: the soup, the electricity bill, the pears. Later, when you come close, each one tells you their own version.

**Il Primo.** He was in the other room. In public he is kind ("Nobody is to blame. Frightened people move very quickly"), which is how the table learns there is something to forgive. In private he stands with his hand flat between the attacker's shoulder blades at the sink, and tells them something (that you have been asking about them on the telephone, that it is not finished), and the attacker looks across the room at you for a long time, and is carrying the knife again. Then he stops behind someone who was not there and says your name and "with his hands". He never says the word.

**No more jumpscares from answers.** A dialogue answer no longer throws a face, a flash, a sting, a colour trip or a "something is still waiting" at the player. Answers still change relationships, motives and what happens later; an answer that lands badly makes the person go quiet, cold-faced, and keep looking at you after the conversation is over.

## Version 28.1 — arms that move like arms

**No more twitching.** Everything that moves an arm in a frame (the pose, a speech gesture, a habit, a cigarette, the table) now produces a target, and the visible joints follow it with a critically damped spring (about 60 ms, no overshoot). The next frame starts again from the target, not from the smoothed pose, so the animation underneath stays continuous; a joint set directly by a cutscene is taken as given. Measured with the whole table talking for 15 seconds: Anna went from 452 frame-to-frame reversals to 3, Walter from 213 to 1, Dario's worst one-frame jump from 86° to 19° (a speech beat).

**The table without the flicker.** The lift that keeps hands out of the tabletop is found by bisection on the smoothed pose, so it changes continuously; it takes hold at once and lets go slowly, and it fades in over the last 10 cm before the edge. Hands in a lap under the table are left alone.

**Habits fade.** Crossed arms, stirring, carving and the rest are let go of over a third of a second when a person starts to gesture or walk, and taken up again over two thirds, instead of vanishing in one frame.

**Il Primo's cigarette hand** no longer takes part in speech gestures, so its fingers hold the cigarette instead of flickering between gesture shapes.

## Version 28 — the game can be finished, and the house wants you dead

**Il Primo is in the slaughterhouse again.** After the dark room he now stands by the hooks until the tea scene, so "speak with Il Primo again" can be done and the game can be finished. He had been hidden there since the first upload, and nothing ever showed him.

**Pressure.** From the default difficulty up, the house no longer waits for Tobia to steal something. After a few quiet minutes with nobody wanting him dead (about 3½ minutes at difficulty 1, less on harder settings, shorter again after the first), somebody decides to, for a reason that needs no theft:
- he will not take a side, and a man who belongs to nobody is the one who will talk (Dario, Walter, Franco, Marta, Gabri);
- they are high on the brown bottle that goes round after the record, and Il Bianco tells them the fourteenth chair has to be emptied (Gabri, Franco, Marta, Dario, Walter).

A visible sign comes first: a line at the table, enormous pupils, a cleaver on the step. Then the existing stalker and trap systems take over, and they can kill. At most four times a game.

**Arms.** Ines's crossed arms are rebuilt so the forearms rest level under the chest, each hand at the opposite elbow, instead of passing through her bust. At the table, elbows, wrists and fingertips are kept on top of the tabletop: every frame the elbow bends further, or draws back to the body, until they clear it. Measured with the whole table talking: before, 61% of hand samples over the table were inside it, by up to 38 cm; after, none.

**The church collapse** is no longer blocked by trees. Trees are still scattered at random, but never in the sightline from the road to the church or in the near foreground of the shot.

**The van** is parked in the farmyard from the first time Tobia has been in the house, never in the opening walk. It becomes Pino's van to open only once he mentions it, and it is solid.

**Oaths**, a few more, still placed by hand and never in a first line: Franco at Anna's coffee, at his own joke and when Tobia says sorry; Gabri shouting at Ines about the thermos; Sergio hauling Gabri to the sink; Pino refusing to wait; Marta at cards; the ambient grumbles of Walter, Gabri and Sergio; and the two new pressure scenes.

## Version 27 — bodies, faces, hands, oaths

**Anatomy from real measurements.** Everyone is rebuilt from anatomical cross-sections instead of capsules (`js/anatomy.js`). Segment lengths follow Drillis & Contini (1966); girths and breadths come from the ANSUR II survey means (neck, chest, waist, hips, thigh, calf, ankle, arm, forearm, wrist, shoulder and hip breadth, for men and for women); muscle contours follow surface anatomy (deltoid cap, biceps and triceps, the forearm's flexor mass and flat wrist, quadriceps with the vastus medialis teardrop, the patella, the calf high on the shank, a narrow ankle, gluteal mass); women's waist-to-hip ratio differs from men's (Singh 1993). Segment volumes land in the real range (thigh ~7.6 L, shank 2.4 L, upper arm 2 L, forearm 1 L, trunk ~44 L). Knees and elbows have joint caps; hands have a palm, four two-segment fingers and an opposable thumb, resting in the natural flexion cascade (Kapandji); shoes are lasts with heel, waist, ball and toe spring. Garments (Il Primo's Perfecto, the leather jackets, rosaries, pins, belts, aprons, tattoos) are refitted to the new trunk and limbs.

**Joints within human limits.** Every pose and gesture is clamped to normal ranges of motion (AAOS values). Arms hang with a few degrees of abduction and palms toward the thighs; an inverted sign had been pushing every resting arm into the hips, and Walter's "measuring" habit swung both arms 70° inward through his own chest. The wrist now twists before it bends, so palms face where they should.

**Faces that show it.** The face is driven by FACS action units (Ekman & Friesen): brows that lift at the inner or outer end or knit (AU1/2/4), upper and lower lids (AU5/6/7), lips in halves whose corners rise, fall or stretch (AU12/15/20), press (AU23), open with the jaw (AU26), and the creases that go with them (forehead lines, frown lines, nasolabial folds). Emotions are Ekman's prototypes (joy, sadness, surprise, fear, anger, disgust, contempt), plus the cult's serene half-smile. What each person shows comes from their temperament, how they stand with Tobia (trust, grievance, fear), whether a death has just happened, and the words they are saying. The body joins in (Wallbott 1998; Dael et al. 2012): sadness drops the head and slumps the chest, fear lifts the shoulders and pulls the arms in, anger leans in.

**Hands that talk.** Speech gestures follow the gesture phrase (preparation, stroke, hold, retraction; Kendon, McNeill) with Italian hand shapes: palm-up open hand, *mano a borsa*, the precision ring, the point (Kendon 2004; Poggi 2002; Müller 2004). Size, speed and frequency follow personality (Neff et al. 2010): Franco wide and two-handed, Ines and Ottavio small and close, Il Primo almost still. Habits are redone anatomically: crossed arms, Anna stirring, Marta carving, Dario's sign of the cross with the right hand (forehead, breast, left shoulder, right shoulder), Walter showing a length between his palms and sighting along his thumb.

**Gabri walks.** When he left the house the stalker loop cleared his walking flag every frame while he was being moved, so his legs never moved. Anyone who is moved now walks, whatever flag the mover set.

**Oaths** (revised after v27, raised in v28). No random swearing: oaths are written into particular lines that are angry, frightened or a joke, never anyone's first line.

## Version 26 — the doorway, the walk, the tragedy

**The front door works when people are about.** The E key targets whatever is closest to the centre of the view; a doorway is aimed at its middle instead of its sill, and a person who is walking past can only be addressed when deliberately centred and close. At a doorway Tobia is facing, a person only wins over the door if they are standing still, clearly nearer and clearly more centred. The prompt always shows what E will do. People leaving the house wait beside the door, out of its line, while Tobia is standing in it.

**Walking.** NPC walking is driven by distance covered, not by a clock, so a planted foot stays planted. The cycle follows the clinical gait cycle (stance ~60% / swing ~40%; heel contact, loading response, foot flat, heel rise and toe-off; knee folding early in swing for clearance), with the ankle path placed on the ground and hip and knee solved by two-bone IK. The pelvis height comes from the legs (a couple of centimetres of rise and fall); arms swing with the opposite leg, slightly behind it, and the thorax counter-rotates against the pelvis (Joseph et al. 2024; Collins, Adamczyk & Kuo 2009). People getting up from the table stand up properly first (feet drawn back, trunk forward, then up), shift their weight, accelerate over the first steps, slow into turns and to a stop. Each person has their own stride, sway, arm swing, lean and tempo.

**Clues no longer kill.** Collecting a clue used to queue the next death; eight clues meant seven deaths. Clues are now only discovery.

**Deaths follow the story.** Each death has its own preconditions (how far the story has come, who is alive, who is needed, what has happened), and deaths are spaced by fresh story between them. I checked the seam in both formats: the largest sample-to-sample jump there is no bigger than elsewhere in the music.
- **Default volume.** The track is normalised to about −18 LUFS (the original was −16.9 LUFS with a 12 LU dynamic range). At the default master and music settings it plays at about −21 LUFS, loud enough to carry over the wind and room tone without covering dialogue. The music slider in Settings (**P**) and the mute key (**M**) work on it as before.
- **Files.** `assets/geatd-loop.ogg` and `assets/geatd-loop.mp3` (the browser picks one); the original `assets/geatd.aiff` is the source. AIFF does not play in Chrome or Firefox, which is why it is not used directly. The deploy script copies `assets/` whole, so the 19 MB source also ships unless you exclude it.

## Run locally

- macOS: double-click `start-local.command`. If it refuses to open, run `chmod +x start-local.command` once in this folder.
- Windows: double-click `start-local.bat`.
- Or run `python3 -m http.server 8765` in this folder and open `http://localhost:8765/`.

Use a current desktop browser with WebGL. Do not open `index.html` as a `file://` document. Three.js 0.160.0 is loaded from a pinned jsDelivr URL, so an Internet connection is required for the engine and credited archival portrait photographs. If those photographs fail, procedural 3D faces remain.

## Publishing with GitHub Pages

1. Upload the repository contents to the root of the repository.
2. In **Settings → Pages**, choose **GitHub Actions** as the source.
3. Push to `main` or `master`, or run the workflow manually from **Actions**.
4. Open the Pages URL after the deployment job succeeds.

The included workflow builds and deploys the site. A local `node_modules` folder is not required.

## Technical notes

- Faces combine procedural geometry with credited archival-photo textures listed in `ASSET_CREDITS.md`.
- NPCs use articulated arms and legs, gaze, blinking and posture shifts.
- Audio is generated in Web Audio: wind, room tone, machinery, chains, hoists, hinges, clocks, pipes, distant knocks and delayed collapse sounds.
- The environment includes industrial yards, canals, winter wheat, maize, waterlogged rice plots, trees, fog, rain, lightning and an accelerated day/night cycle.
- Add `?lowfx` to the URL to disable the depth-of-field lens effect on slower machines.
- No analytics or browser fingerprint collection is included. The browser may show a generic anti-fingerprinting notice because the game uses WebGL and Canvas for rendering and textures.
